SECTION 1 — OVERVIEW
================================================================================

The Vicharanashala Summership 2026 (also referred to as VINS — Vicharanashala
Internship) is a two-month, free, online internship programme run by the
Vicharanashala Lab for Education Design (VLED) at the Indian Institute of
Technology (IIT) Ropar. The programme involves real open-source software
contributions under the mentorship of the Vicharanashala research and
engineering team. The certificate upon successful completion is issued by the
Vicharanashala Lab for Education Design at IIT Ropar.

The programme is open to currently-enrolled undergraduate, postgraduate, and
doctoral students at any recognised college or university. Working professionals
and candidates who have already graduated without current enrolment are not
eligible. There is no stipend; however, stellar performers may receive a
discretionary stipend at the lab's discretion.

The primary management and communication platform for the internship is
samagama.in, where students interact with the AI-powered chat assistant named
Yaksha, submit required documents, confirm internship dates, and access offer
letters and other official communications.

This knowledge base consolidates all programme FAQ content with an analytical
framework derived from 13 days (May 11–23, 2026) of WhatsApp troubleshoot
group data (5,437 chat lines, 227 media files), identifying systemic support
gaps and recommending technology-driven solutions.

================================================================================

SECTION 3 — PROGRAMME STRUCTURE AND PHASES
================================================================================

3.1 Four-Phase Structure with Badges

VINS is organized into four phases, each represented by a badge:

Bronze Phase (Phase 1):
A short training period at the start of the internship, calibrated to the
student's existing knowledge. If a student already possesses foundational
competency, the mentor may advance the student directly to the Silver phase.
During Bronze, all students complete mandatory coursework on the ViBe Learning
Management System (LMS). This includes a MERN Stack course (waivable for
returning VLED interns) and the new AI Fundamentals course (Fundamentals of AI
using an Agriculture Dataset), which is mandatory for all participants including
returning interns.

Silver Phase (Phase 2):
The main internship work phase. Students contribute to a real open-source
project under a Vicharanashala mentor. Completing both Bronze and Silver earns
the internship certificate. Silver requires team-based work; all teams are fixed
at four members from different institutions.

Gold Phase (Phase 3):
A recognition awarded during Silver if the student's contribution constitutes a
meaningful, standalone feature rather than a minor fix. Gold is not guaranteed;
it is awarded based on the quality and independence of the contribution.

Platinum Phase (Phase 4):
A standing invitation to visit the IIT Ropar lab for a short trip at any time
during the year following internship completion. Vicharanashala provides a small
visit stipend to assist with travel. Platinum is an additional recognition, not
a default outcome.

Most interns complete at the Bronze plus Silver level, which is the standard
certificate outcome. Gold and Platinum are awarded based on merit.

3.2 Programme Comparison: VINS vs. VISE

VINS (Vicharanashala Internship — Online): Online programme. No fellowship or
stipend. Certificate is identical to VISE.

VISE (Vicharanashala Internship — Offline): On-campus programme at IIT Ropar.
Includes a fellowship. Fixed capacity determined by mentor bandwidth, hostel
availability, and stipend allocation.

The two tracks are finalized at the interview stage. Candidates cannot switch
between tracks after selection. The certificate does not distinguish whether
completion was online or on-campus.

3.3 Distinction from IIT Ropar's Official Internship

Summership 2026 is a VLED Lab initiative, not IIT Ropar's centrally-run
institutional summer research internship. The certificate is issued by the
Vicharanashala Lab for Education Design, not by IIT Ropar centrally. Students
must not represent the two programmes as equivalent.

SECTION 5 — TIMING, DATES, AND COHORT STRUCTURE
================================================================================

5.1 Programme Duration and Deadline

The internship lasts two months from the student's chosen start date, with an
optional one-month grace period. The absolute hard deadline for completion is
31 December 2026. No exceptions are made to this end date.

5.2 Start Date Flexibility

VINS allows flexible start dates within 2026. However, the programme strongly
recommends starting as early as possible (May–July) for three reasons:

Cohort Networking: The main cohort progresses through Bronze together. Peer
discussions, parallel problem-solving, and lasting connections are concentrated
in this window. Students who start later are largely working solo.

Teaching Assistant (TA) Support: TAs are full-time during May–July. After this
period, TAs return to their own college work and their availability is
significantly reduced.

Training Rollout: Training material is delivered with cohort-wide discussion
during the main window, not as static standalone documents for late starters.

5.3 Exam Conflicts and Leave Policy

Leave is not permitted during the internship. If a student is concurrently
attending classes or sitting examinations, the student will be relieved
immediately and invited to join the next batch.

Students with exams during the main cohort window must defer their start date to
after their exams conclude and then opt in. Attempting to juggle the internship
with ongoing exams is explicitly prohibited. If the programme later discovers
that a candidate was sitting college exams during the internship period, the
internship may be terminated or the certificate withheld, including after
otherwise completing the programme.

No relaxation or exemption is offered for exams scheduled during the internship
window. The 55-day continuous attendance requirement is non-negotiable.

5.4 Late Starters

Students who start after the main May–July cohort can still complete the
internship and earn the certificate, provided they finish by 31 December 2026.
However, late starters will not have access to orientation recordings (see
Section 10.3 for details on the mandatory proctored catch-up path), will
experience reduced TA support, and will miss the cohort networking effect.

SECTION 6 — NO OBJECTION CERTIFICATE (NOC)
================================================================================

6.1 Purpose

The NOC is institutional consent that the student is permitted to commit their
time to the internship. It is required before the formal offer letter is issued.

6.2 Date Range for NOC

The NOC should cover the student's chosen start date through the end date (start
plus two months, with up to one month grace), ensuring the end date is on or
before 31 December 2026. The start date on the NOC must match the dates the
student confirms on the samagama.in dashboard.

6.3 Authorised Signatories

Any of the following officers at the student's institution may sign the NOC:
Head of Department (HOD), Acting HOD (during holiday periods), Principal, Dean,
Director, or Training and Placement Officer.

6.4 Required Elements on NOC

Whether using the Vicharanashala printable format or the college's own format,
the NOC must contain all four of the following elements:
1. The authorised signatory's handwritten signature (most critical).
2. The authorised signatory's official institutional email address (used for
   cross-verification).
3. The student's full name.
4. The student's signature.

Additionally, when using the Vicharanashala printable NOC format, an
institutional rubber stamp or seal must be applied in the designated area.
Digital signatures are not accepted on the PDF path.

6.5 Submission Method — PDF Path

Students download the blank NOC from the samagama.in dashboard using the
"Download blank NOC" button, print it, obtain handwritten signature and
institutional stamp, scan it, and upload the signed PDF using the "Upload signed
NOC (PDF)" button. The file must be a PDF of at most 1 MB.

6.6 Submission Method — Email Forward Path (Equivalent Alternative)

If a physical printout is impractical, the HOD may use the email-forward path:
1. Student downloads the "text NOC (email path)" from the dashboard.
2. Student fills in the student-side fields and emails the document to the HOD.
3. The HOD forwards the email to sudarshan@iitrpr.ac.in from their official
   institutional email address (not a personal Gmail or Outlook account).
4. Subject line must be exactly: "NOC for my student [Student Full Name]".
The forward itself counts as the signature. The HOD's official institutional
email domain serves as the verification, equivalent to the signatory-email field
on the PDF NOC.

6.7 Verification Timeline

NOC verification typically takes anywhere from one hour to one full working day
after upload.

6.8 Tentative Offer Letter Route

If the student's start date is approaching and NOC verification has not
completed, the student may upload a self-declaration on their profile at
samagama.in. A tentative offer letter on Vicharanashala letterhead is issued
immediately. The formal offer letter is issued once the NOC clears verification.
Both the tentative and formal offer letters are valid institutional documents.

6.9 HOD Requires Written Confirmation Before Signing

If the HOD requires a document before signing the NOC, the student should use
the tentative offer letter route: upload a self-declaration, receive the
tentative offer letter, and present it to the HOD. The HOD then signs the NOC,
which the student uploads to receive the formal offer letter.

6.10 Online-Course-Only Students

If the student's only academic engagement is an online-only course (Masai,
NPTEL, Coursera, Udacity, or a bootcamp) without concurrent enrollment in a
full-time degree programme, the internship is not open to that student in this
cycle. If the student is concurrently enrolled in a full-time degree programme,
they should obtain the NOC from that institution.

6.11 NOC Upload Locations on Dashboard

The NOC section appears in three places on the samagama.in interface:
1. A compact pill in the dark header bar at the top of every screen.
2. A standalone NOC card on the dashboard, between the Results card and the
   Talk-to-Yaksha button.
3. A NOC section at the bottom of the full Result message.
All three locations are backed by the same endpoints. The chat surface no longer
carries any NOC affordance; students must use the dashboard buttons.

SECTION 7 — SELECTION, OFFER LETTER, AND ACCEPTANCE
================================================================================

7.1 Confirming Selection

A student is selected when a yellow VINS result panel is visible on their
samagama.in dashboard. No separate selection confirmation email is sent.

7.2 Opting Into VINS

To formally opt in, the student tells Yaksha in the chat: "I want to take up
the online internship without stipend." Yaksha confirms. No separate
confirmation email is sent at this stage.

7.3 Confirming Internship Dates

After opting in via Yaksha, the student logs in to samagama.in and accesses the
"Confirm your internship dates" card on the dashboard. Date pickers pre-fill
with sensible defaults. The student adjusts if needed and saves. The end date
must be on or before 31 December 2026. The student can edit dates from this same
card at any time before the offer letter is generated.

7.4 Offer Letter Issuance

Formal offer letter: Issued automatically once (a) the signed NOC has been
verified and (b) the student has confirmed internship dates on the dashboard.
Issuance typically occurs within one hour of the second step completing.

Tentative offer letter: Issued immediately upon upload of a self-declaration
if the student cannot wait for NOC verification. The formal offer letter follows
once NOC verification clears.

The offer letter is available for download from the dashboard at samagama.in,
not from email. A notification email from no-reply@vicharanashala.ai (subject:
"Your Vicharanashala Offer Letter is ready — please log in to download") is sent
as a notification only. The actual document must be downloaded from the dashboard.

7.5 Accepting the Offer Letter

The acceptance must follow a precise format. The student must Reply All on the
offer-letter email thread (which already contains sudarshan@iitrpr.ac.in) and
paste the following statement exactly:

  "I, [Full Name], confirm that I have read, understood, and accepted all terms,
  conditions, and obligations set out in this offer letter and in the program FAQ
  at samagama.in. I formally accept the offer of Summer Internship 2026."

Single-word slips and obvious typing mistakes in an otherwise complete statement
are tolerated. The reply must arrive within 5 days of the offer letter being
sent.

Alternatively, the student may download the offer letter PDF, fill in their name
and date in the acceptance block, sign and scan it as a PDF, and attach it to
the reply.

7.6 Non-Compliant Acceptance

If the acceptance does not follow the stated format exactly (paraphrasing,
missing the date, missing the FAQ reference, bare "I accept"), the offer is
withdrawn immediately with no further correspondence. The withdrawal is final.

7.7 Appeal Process for Withdrawn Offers

The student must send a fresh email (not a reply to the withdrawal) to
sudarshansudarshan@gmail.com with the subject line exactly:
"Request to Reconsider: Confirmation Reply Error"

The subject line must be copied exactly; any deviation causes the AI routing
engine to miss the appeal. If the reason stated is genuine, a response is
provided within 24 hours. A successful appeal places the student on a separate
track requiring completion of a short attention-to-detail course before the
internship proceeds.

7.8 Dashboard and Acceptance Tracking

The samagama.in dashboard tracks NOC status, internship dates, and the offer
letter. It does not track the acceptance email. No dashboard update occurs after
the acceptance is sent. This is expected behaviour.

7.9 Changing Internship Dates

Before offer letter generation: The student edits dates freely from the
"Confirm Internship Dates" card on the dashboard.

After offer letter generation: The dates are locked on the dashboard. To change
them, the NOC signing authority from the student's institution must email:
  To: harshdeep.r@vicharanashala.ai
  CC: sudarshan@iitrpr.ac.in
  Subject: "Date change request — [Student Full Name] — Vicharanashala
   Summership 2026"
  Body: Reason for change and new proposed start and end dates.
  Attachment: A justification letter written by the student, signed by the NOC
   approver.

The email must originate from the NOC approver's institutional address. Student-
sent requests are not accepted at this stage. Once processed, the Confirm
Internship Dates card is unlocked and the offer letter is re-issued.

7.10 Certificate

Every intern who completes both Bronze and Silver phases receives an
e-certificate issued by Vicharanashala, IIT Ropar. The certificate is
downloaded from the dashboard. No physical copies are mailed. The certificate
does not specify whether the internship was completed online or on-campus. It
is digitally signed and verifiable using the certificate number.

Vicharanashala does not send grade reports or evaluations to universities. The
certificate is the document for college or placement office submission.

SECTION 8 — COMMUNICATION CHANNELS AND CODE OF CONDUCT
================================================================================

8.1 Official Channels

All programme communication must occur through official channels only:

Yaksha Chat on samagama.in: Primary channel for any query. Start a message
with #escalate to escalate to a human support agent.

Email (Outbound Only): Notifications are sent FROM no-reply@vicharanashala.ai.
This is an outbound-only address — replies to this address are not read. For
direct team contact (only when Yaksha genuinely cannot help), students email
sudarshansudarshan@gmail.com.

Moderated WhatsApp Troubleshooting Group: Vicharanashala invites students to
this group after their offer letter is issued. This is the only WhatsApp group
operated by Vicharanashala.

8.2 Prohibited Channels

Creating, joining, or operating any unofficial group — including parallel
WhatsApp groups, Telegram channels, Discord servers, or any peer-coordinated
space that purports to represent Vicharanashala, Samagama, or the internship
cohort — is strictly prohibited. This prohibition applies regardless of the
group's name, description, or stated affiliation.

Candidates found to be running or actively participating in such unofficial
channels face consequences including but not limited to: withdrawal of the offer
letter, removal from the internship, blacklisting from future Vicharanashala
programmes, and formal communication to the student's college or placement
office.

Exception: A four-person team WhatsApp or email thread limited to project
teammates for internal coordination is acceptable under Section 13.23. This is
distinct from cohort-wide or programme-wide unofficial groups.

8.3 No Discord Server

Vicharanashala does not operate a Discord server. Any Discord server claiming
to represent the programme is unofficial.

8.4 No WhatsApp Broadcast Group by Default

There is no general WhatsApp group for all candidates. The moderated
troubleshooting group (invite-only, post offer letter) is the only WhatsApp
channel operated by Vicharanashala.

8.5 Kickoff Orientation

A kickoff orientation is held for the main summer cohort (candidates starting in
the May–July window). The Zoom link is delivered via email to the registered
samagama.in address and through the Yaksha chat portal.

For students starting after the main window, there is no separate kickoff event.
Late starters must complete orientation through a special proctored catch-up
path on ViBe. The catch-up is fully proctored and includes quizzes. Completion
of the catch-up is mandatory before participating in regular daily standups.
Orientation session recordings are not shared with interns.

8.6 Daily Zoom Standups

Daily standup links are posted in the Announcements section on the samagama.in
dashboard (accessible via the announcement bell at the top of the page).
Vicharanashala does not send separate emails for daily standups. Attendance at
daily standups is mandatory for all interns.

SECTION 9 — WORK, MENTORSHIP, AND PROJECTS
================================================================================

9.1 Project Assignment

Students are assigned to a real open-source project from the Vicharanashala
portfolio. Assignment is based on the student's background and the lab's current
needs. Project areas include AI/ML, web development, NLP, computer vision,
agriculture-tech (Annam.AI project), education-tech (ViBe platform), and open-
source infrastructure. The specific project is not pre-announced; students
accept assignment upon joining.

9.2 Workload

The internship requires 6 to 10 hours of focused work per day, sometimes more
during the build phase. It is a full-time commitment for the two-month duration.
Most dropouts occur among candidates attempting to juggle other commitments
simultaneously.

9.3 Mentorship Model

The mentorship model is fluid and mirrors real open-source collaboration. A
student may work with a senior researcher, a peer, or a subject-matter expert
depending on the question or task. Mentor assignment occurs when the student
completes Bronze phase coursework and moves into Phase 2 (Silver). Mentors do
not reach out on Day 1 of the internship.

9.4 Equipment Requirements

Students must have a personal laptop. Linux or macOS is preferred. Windows users
must install a Unix-compatible terminal environment such as Windows Subsystem
for Linux (WSL) or a tool such as PuTTY, as SSH and Unix-style command-line
operations are integral to the work. No fixed software preload list is
maintained; the assigned mentor will guide on specific tooling requirements.

9.5 Email and Platform Identity

The student's registered samagama.in email is the sole identifier across all
programme platforms, including GitHub, Zoom, and ViBe. Progress tracking,
mentor assignment, and certificate issuance are all tied to this email. Using a
different email on any platform cannot be retroactively corrected.

Exception for ViBe: ViBe requires a Gmail address for signup. If the registered
samagama.in email is not a Gmail address (for example, a college email), the
student may use a personal Gmail for ViBe and must notify Yaksha using the tag:
  "#vibe-email [gmail-address]"
This links the ViBe record to the samagama.in record.

SECTION 10 — VIBЕ LEARNING MANAGEMENT SYSTEM (VIBE LMS)
================================================================================

10.1 About ViBe

ViBe (https://vibe.vicharanashala.ai/auth) is the Learning Management System
used during Phase 1 (Bronze) for mandatory coursework. It is a free and open-
source platform built and improved by interns and students. The platform features
proctored learning with real-time monitoring via camera and microphone.

10.2 Course Registration

1. Click the AI Fundamentals registration link from the Phase 1 launch email.
2. Sign in or create a ViBe account using the registered Gmail address.
3. Open the registration link again after logging in — the second click triggers
   enrollment.
4. Complete the registration form and submit.
5. The course appears immediately on the ViBe dashboard.

10.3 Proctoring on ViBe

ViBe's proctoring system is modular. Each check — face visibility, single face
in frame, adequate lighting, absence of background voices, gaze on screen, and
camera/microphone permissions — can be independently enabled or disabled by the
instructor for each specific course or cohort. Students must not assume proctoring
rules from one course apply to another course on the same platform.

The five real-time checks when proctoring is fully enabled:
1. A face is visible in the camera frame.
2. Only one face is in the frame.
3. There is sufficient lighting on the face.
4. The room does not contain sustained background voices.
5. The student's gaze is directed at the screen.

Brief and normal movements (stretching, sneezing, glancing at a notebook) are
acceptable. Only sustained anomalous patterns trigger intervention.

ViBe does not continuously record video of students. The camera and microphone
are used for real-time presence checks only. Long recordings of faces or voices
are not stored.

10.4 Linear Progression

All ViBe courses enforce linear progression. Students must complete each video
and quiz in the exact order displayed in the left panel before the next item
unlocks. Clicking ahead through the left panel triggers an "Access Restricted"
alert, which is intentional and not a bug.

To resolve "Access Restricted": scroll through the left panel from the beginning,
find any item without a completion tick, complete it, and refresh the page.

10.5 Quiz Formats

ViBe quizzes appear in four formats:
- Multiple Choice Question (MCQ): One correct answer from several options.
- Multiple Select Question (MSQ): One or more correct options; "select all
  that apply."
- Numerical Answer Type (NAT): A text box for a numeric value; enter only the
  number without units unless explicitly requested.
- True or False: A statement with two options; single qualifier words such as
  "always," "never," or "only" can change the correct answer.

10.6 Re-Watch Mechanism

If a check-in quiz answer does not register correctly, ViBe returns the student
to the clip to attempt again. Re-watches are not recorded against the student;
they do not affect Health Points (HP) or evaluation scores.

10.7 Penalty Scores

Penalty scores are generated when anomalies are detected during video lessons or
quizzes. High penalty scores may require the student to rewatch the video and
retake the quiz. As of the current cycle, penalty scores do not affect HP or
overall performance evaluation; their purpose is to ensure genuine engagement.

10.8 Progress Saving

Progress is saved server-side, tied to the registered email. Clearing the
browser cache, refreshing, switching browsers, or reinstalling the browser does
not erase progress. Logging back in with the same registered email restores all
completed clips and quizzes.

10.9 Troubleshooting Video Issues

In order: refresh the page; inspect the browser console for errors; log out and
log in again; try a different browser; clear browsing data and cache and re-
login. If the issue persists, record the issue and contact Yaksha using the tag
"#escalate-ViBe."

10.10 Recommended Study Environment

Optimal ViBe session setup:
- Light source in front of the face, not behind (a window behind causes
  silhouetting and triggers presence check failures).
- Only the registered student in the camera frame.
- Quiet environment — no television, music with words, or other people on calls.
- Stay on the ViBe browser tab during lessons; switching tabs or remaining idle
  may restart the video.
- Study in silence during lessons; discussing content aloud can trigger the
  background-voice check.

10.11 Recommended Daily Rhythm

Daily consistent short sessions work better than infrequent marathon sessions.
A practical target is approximately 3.33% course completion per day for cohorts
with defined deadlines. Specific milestone schedules communicated by the
programme team take precedence over this general guideline.

10.12 Proctored Special Examination Path

Students who strongly believe the standard ViBe workflow does not fairly reflect
their understanding may request a formal alternative evaluation. This path is
intentionally rigorous:
- Watch specified YouTube content completely.
- Sit a three-hour proctored examination under two-camera supervision (front
  and side view) with a live online human proctor.
- Scoring: below 60% = not qualified, must return to standard ViBe mode;
  60–80% = one further attempt at the next scheduled exam; above 80% = passed.
The special exam is scheduled once per fortnight. This path is typically slower
and more demanding than completing the standard ViBe workflow.

10.13 ViBe Consent

Providing consent for camera and microphone access is mandatory. Students who
decline cannot proceed with the course. ViBe's proctoring consent is a core
participation requirement.

10.14 Flag Feature

The Flag feature on ViBe is intended only for course-content issues such as
problems with video content or quiz questions. Technical issues, platform errors,
login problems, and logistics queries must be directed to Yaksha, not the Flag
feature.

10.15 Exemptions for Returning VLED Interns

Students who previously interned with VLED and completed the MERN Stack
coursework do not need to repeat it. To claim this exemption, the student types
"#exemption from mern stack course" in the Yaksha chat on samagama.in.

The AI Fundamentals course is new in this cycle and is mandatory for all
participants, including returning interns. The broader viva-route exemption from
all coursework is available only to first-time interns with demonstrated
competency in both stacks; it is not available to returning interns.

10.16 Live Session Attendance

Live sessions are mandatory for every intern regardless of track (coursework,
MERN-exempt, or viva-route). The daily schedule is communicated via any of the
three channels: the Samagama dashboard, Yaksha chat, or email.

SECTION 11 — TEAM FORMATION (PHASE 2)
================================================================================

11.1 Structure

All Phase 2 and Phase 3 projects are completed in teams. Team size is fixed at
exactly four members. Teams with fewer than four members will be expanded by
administrative assignment. Teams cannot exceed four members.

11.2 Formation Method

Students who joined on May 15 and 16 formed teams through a structured activity.
Students joining later are randomly assigned to teams by the administration.
Students who were present on May 15/16 but could not form a team during the
activity are also randomly assigned.

11.3 Composition Rules

Teams must consist of members from different institutions, to encourage
cross-institutional networking. Students from the same institution at different
campuses or locations may be allowed in exceptional cases.

Students may not form teams with members of their existing cohort groups (for
example, IIT MBS cohort members should not team with other IIT MBS members).

11.4 Team Assignment Finality

Once teams are finalized and locked, assignments cannot be changed, dissolved,
or reformed. Requests for specific teammates after assignment are not
entertained. Project assignments are also final; students allocated to a project
other than their first preference cannot request reallocation.

11.5 Inactivity and Departures

If a team member becomes inactive or ineligible, the administration attempts to
assign a replacement. If no replacement is available, the remaining team may
continue as a team of three. Concerns about inactive team members must be
reported to the mentor/scholar immediately.

11.6 Team Communication

Teams are expected to self-organize internal communication (for example, a
four-person WhatsApp or email thread limited to project teammates). This in-team
coordination is distinct from the prohibited cohort-wide unofficial channels
described in Section 8.2.

11.7 Mentor Assignment

The assigned mentor is the scholar responsible for the project the team is
working on. Team details are announced in phases via email. Students not
included in a team announcement by the time their cohort moves to team
activities should raise the issue through Yaksha or contact a scholar.

SECTION 12 — ROSETTA INTERNSHIP JOURNAL
================================================================================

12.1 What Rosetta Is

Rosetta is a 65-day internship journal — one entry per day for the full duration
of Summership 2026. Students write in it daily, keep it privately throughout the
internship, and submit it as one of the required completion criteria.

The name references the Rosetta Stone, discovered in 1799, which carried the
same text in three scripts and became the key to decoding ancient Egyptian
hieroglyphics. The journal is meant to serve a similar purpose: consistent,
honest daily writing that becomes the key to understanding one's own learning
experience.

12.2 Purpose

For the student: builds the ability to articulate what was learned, what
changed, and what could be done differently — an articulation that intense
experience alone rarely produces.

For Vicharanashala: provides qualitative insight into the student experience
that no survey can capture, used to improve future cohorts.

12.3 Daily Thinking Routines

Each day's entry has a thinking routine — a short framework providing a specific
lens for reflection. Examples:
- 3-2-1: Three things engaged with, two questions on the student's mind, one
  surprise.
- Muddy/Clear: What is sharp, what is still foggy.
- What? So What? Now What?: Separate facts from meaning from action.

Routines rotate across the 65 days to prevent mechanical repetition. No prior
research or preparation is needed; the student reads the description at the top
of each day's entry and writes.

12.4 Obtaining and Using Rosetta

The journal is shared as a Google Doc template link during orientation. Students
make a copy to their own Google Drive and rename it:
"Rosetta — [Student Name] — Summership 2026"

Students must not write in the shared template. The daily process is: open the
personal copy, scroll to today's entry, fill in the date, read the thinking
routine, answer the three prompts, and close the document. Each entry should
take 10 to 20 minutes.

12.5 Entry Standards

No minimum or maximum word count applies. Three to five sentences per prompt is
typical. Honesty and specificity matter more than length. One-word answers,
copy-pasted text, vague non-answers, and AI-generated entries are not acceptable.
Using any AI tool (ChatGPT, Claude, or similar) to write journal entries is
prohibited. Entries that read as AI-generated will not count toward the
completion requirement.

12.6 Missing Entries

Missed entries should be filled in as soon as possible. The student should write
the actual date of filling, note that the entry is late, and explain why. Late
honest entries are always preferable to blank entries.

12.7 Privacy

Vicharanashala does not access the journal during the 65 days. The journal is
read only after submission at the end of the internship.

12.8 Submission

On or before Day 65, the student shares the Rosetta Google Doc with the
programme coordinator's email address (shared during wrap-up week) with Viewer
permission. The document title must include the student's name, all 65 entries
must be filled, and the cover page must include the student's name, product, and
team. An incomplete or AI-generated journal is not accepted.

Workflow: NOC Submission
 
Current State:       Opted in via Yaksha
Expected Next State: NOC Download Option Appears on Dashboard
Expected Delay:      2–3 days after opt-in (rolling batch processing)
Escalate If:         NOC download option does not appear after 5 days
 
What is an NOC:
An NOC (No Objection Certificate) is a document from the student's college or
university confirming that the institution has no objection to the student
participating in the Vicharanashala internship during the stated period.
 
Step 1 — Download the Blank NOC:
Once the NOC section appears on the samagama.in dashboard, the student clicks
"Download blank NOC." The NOC format is provided; students do not need to
design or draft their own format.
 
Step 2 — Fill in Dates Carefully:
The NOC dates must match the internship dates the student will confirm on the
dashboard. The internship lasts two months. The end date must be on or before
31 December 2026. Date mismatches between the NOC and the dashboard create
processing issues and delays.
 
Step 3 — Obtain Signature:
Authorised signatories: HOD, Acting HOD (during holiday periods), Principal,
Dean, Director, or Training and Placement Officer. For dual-degree students,
either institution may sign.
 
Required elements on the NOC (any format):
1. Handwritten signature of the authorised signatory (most important).
2. Official institutional email address of the signatory (used for
   cross-verification).
3. Student's full name.
4. Student's signature.
When using the Vicharanashala printable format: an institutional rubber stamp or
seal must also be applied. Digital signatures are not accepted.
 
Alternative — Email Forward Path (equivalent to signed PDF):
If a physical printout is impractical, the HOD may use the email-forward path.
1. Student downloads the "text NOC (email path)" from the dashboard.
2. Student fills in student-side fields and sends the document to the HOD.
3. The HOD forwards the email to sudarshan@iitrpr.ac.in from their official
   institutional email address (not a personal Gmail or Outlook account).
4. Subject line must be exactly: "NOC for my student [Student Full Name]"
The HOD's forward from their institutional email constitutes the signature.
 
Step 4 — Upload the Signed NOC:
Once the "Upload signed NOC (PDF)" button appears on the dashboard, the student
uploads the signed and scanned NOC. File must be a PDF of at most 1 MB.
 
Step 5 — Wait for Verification:
NOC verification typically takes one hour to one full working day.
 
Current State:       NOC Uploaded
Expected Next State: NOC Verified → Offer Letter Issued
Expected Delay:      1 hour to 1 working day (verification) + up to 1 hour
                     (offer letter generation)
Escalate If:         NOC shows no verification update after 3 working days
 
Alternate Path — Tentative Offer Letter:
If the student's internship start date is approaching and NOC verification has
not completed, the student may upload a self-declaration on their samagama.in
profile. A tentative offer letter is issued immediately. The formal offer letter
replaces it once NOC verification clears. Both documents are valid.

- Is the NOC signed with a handwritten signature (not digital)?
- Does the NOC include the signatory's official institutional email address?
- Have you uploaded the signed NOC using the dashboard upload button?
- Was the uploaded file a PDF and under 1 MB?

CHUNK-015 | VIBE LMS — ACCESS AND COMMON ISSUES
Topic: Registering, accessing, and troubleshooting the ViBe learning platform
================================================================================
 
What ViBe Is:
ViBe (https://vibe.vicharanashala.ai/auth) is the Learning Management System
used during Phase 1 (Bronze) for mandatory coursework. It is built and maintained
by interns and students as a free, open-source platform.
 
Registration Steps:
1. Click the AI Fundamentals registration link sent in the Phase 1 launch email.
2. Go to the ViBe sign-in page and create an account, or log in if already
   registered.
3. ViBe requires a Gmail address. If the registered samagama.in email is not
   Gmail, use any personal Gmail and notify Yaksha with the tag:
   "#vibe-email [gmail-address]"
4. After logging in, open the registration link again in the same browser.
   The second click after login is what enrols the student.
5. Complete the registration form and submit. The course appears on the dashboard.
 
Email Mismatch:
If the student registered on ViBe with a different email than the samagama.in
registered email, the student must notify Yaksha using "#vibe-email [email]" so
the records can be linked manually.
 
Common Issue — "No course enrolled" after accepting invite:
1. Confirm you are logged in with the correct registered email.
2. Check for multiple email aliases; use the exact registered email.
3. Log out and log in again.
4. Use personal Wi-Fi instead of college Wi-Fi (college networks may restrict
   access).
5. Enable third-party cookies in the browser: go to browser cookie settings and
   allow cookies from vicharanashala.ai.
6. Change laptop DNS to Google DNS (8.8.8.8 preferred, 8.8.4.4 alternate).
7. Flush DNS cache (run ipconfig /flushdns in Command Prompt as administrator).
8. Restart Wi-Fi and retry.
 
Common Issue — Videos stuck, looping, or restarting:
- Stay on the ViBe browser tab throughout. Switching tabs may restart the video.
- Keep the camera and microphone permissions enabled at all times.
- Ensure sufficient lighting — the face should be clearly visible, not silhouetted.
- Reduce background noise — voices in the room may trigger a restart.
- Do not skip ahead; videos must be watched in full sequence.
 
Common Issue — "Access Restricted" red banner:
This is not a bug. It appears when the student attempts to open a video or quiz
without completing all earlier items. ViBe returns the student to the last
legitimately completed item. To resolve: scroll through the left panel from the
beginning, find the first item without a completion tick, complete it, and refresh.
 
Progress Saving:
Progress is saved on the server. Clearing browser cache, refreshing, or switching
browsers does not erase progress. Log back in with the registered email to
restore all progress.
 
Escalation for ViBe Issues:
If all troubleshooting steps fail, contact Yaksha with the tag "#escalate-ViBe"
and include a description of the issue and any error messages visible in the
browser console.
 
Consent Requirement:
Camera and microphone consent is mandatory. Students who decline cannot proceed
with the ViBe course. ViBe does not continuously record video; the camera is used
for real-time presence checks only.

CHUNK-017 | LEAVE, ATTENDANCE, AND EXAM CONFLICTS
Topic: Attendance rules and what to do about exam conflicts
================================================================================
 
Attendance Rule:
Leave is not permitted during the internship. The internship requires 6 to 10
hours of focused work per day. This is a full-time commitment.
 
Exam Conflict Rule:
If a student has upcoming exams that cannot be avoided during the internship
window, the student must defer their start date until after the exams conclude.
Attempting to juggle exams and the internship simultaneously is not permitted.
 
If the programme discovers that a student was sitting college exams during the
internship period, the internship may be terminated or the certificate withheld,
including after otherwise completing the programme.
 
No Exemptions:
No leave, exemptions, relaxation periods, or exam windows are available during
the internship. The 55-day continuous attendance requirement is non-negotiable.
 
Late Start Option:
A student with unavoidable exam conflicts may start after their exams conclude,
provided the end date (start plus two months, or three months with grace) falls
on or before 31 December 2026.
 
Classes During Internship:
If a student is simultaneously attending college classes or exams, the student
will be relieved from the internship and invited to join the next batch.

CHUNK-018 | TEAM FORMATION — RULES AND PROCESS
Topic: How teams are formed and managed in Phase 2
================================================================================
 
Team Size: Exactly four members. Fixed. Cannot be reduced below four or
increased above four at the time of final formation.
 
Formation Method:
- Students who joined May 15–16: Teams formed through a structured activity.
- Students joining later: Teams randomly assigned by the administration.
- Students on May 15–16 who could not form a team during the activity: Randomly
  assigned.
 
Team Composition Rules:
- Teams must include members from different institutions.
- Students from the same institution at different campuses may be permitted.
- Students may not team with members of their existing cohort groups (e.g., IIT
  MBS cohort members should not team with other IIT MBS members).
 
Finality of Assignments:
Team assignments are final once locked. No switches, dissolutions, or team
reformations are permitted. Requests for specific teammates are not entertained.
Project assignments are also final; reallocation to a different project is not
possible.
 
If a Team Member Leaves:
Report immediately to the mentor or scholar. The administration attempts to
assign a replacement. If no replacement is found, the team may continue as
three members.
 
Inactive Team Members:
Report inactivity to the mentor or scholar early. Prolonged inactivity may
result in administrative intervention.
 
Team Communication:
Teams are expected to self-organise internal communication (a four-person
WhatsApp or email thread is acceptable). Official programme updates continue
through email and Yaksha chat on samagama.in.
 
Team Name Changes:
Team names are tentative and can be changed, but frequent changes are
discouraged. If multiple teams share the same name, suffixes are applied
(e.g., Team X-1, Team X-2).
 
When Team Activities Begin:
Team-based work begins in Phase 2 (Silver). During Phase 1 (Bronze, coursework),
no team coordination is required.

CHUNK-019 | ROSETTA JOURNAL — RULES AND REQUIREMENTS
Topic: The mandatory 65-day internship journal
================================================================================
 
What Rosetta Is:
Rosetta is a mandatory 65-day internship journal. One entry per day for the full
duration of Summership 2026. Students write privately, keep the document
themselves, and submit it at the end as one of the completion requirements.
 
How to Obtain It:
The journal is shared as a Google Doc template link during orientation. The
student makes a copy to their own Google Drive and renames it:
"Rosetta — [Student Name] — Summership 2026"
Students must write in their own copy, not the shared template.
 
Daily Process:
1. Open the personal Rosetta Google Doc.
2. Scroll to today's day number.
3. Fill in the date.
4. Read the thinking routine name and description.
5. Answer the three prompts.
The process should take 10 to 20 minutes per day.
 
Entry Standards:
Entries must be honest, specific, and written in the student's own voice. Three
to five sentences per prompt is typical. One-word answers, vague non-answers,
copy-pasted text, and AI-generated entries are not acceptable and will not be
counted toward the completion requirement.
 
AI Tools:
Using any AI tool (including ChatGPT, Claude, or similar) to write Rosetta
entries is strictly prohibited. Entries that read as AI-generated will not be
accepted.
 
Missed Entries:
Fill in missed entries as soon as possible. Write the actual date of filling,
note that the entry is late, and explain why. Late honest entries are preferable
to blank entries.
 
Submission:
On or before Day 65, share the Rosetta Google Doc with the programme
coordinator's email address (provided during wrap-up week) with Viewer permission.
Ensure: the document title includes the student's name, all 65 entries are
filled, and the cover page shows name, product, and team.
 
Rosetta submission is a required completion criterion for the certificate.
